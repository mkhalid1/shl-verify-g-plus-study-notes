# FAQ

These are the questions people search for most about the SHL general ability test, taken from Google results for searches such as "shl verify g+ test" and "shl general ability test" in November 2026. Answers use SHL's own pages where SHL has published something, and say so when it has not.

## What is the SHL Verify G+ test?

A general ability test from SHL that measures numerical, deductive and inductive reasoning in one sitting. SHL says it measures general mental ability, or "G". See the [test overview](test-overview.md).

## Is the SHL general ability test the same as Verify G+?

Often, but not always. "General ability" is the plain-English name, and Verify G+ is the multiple-choice product. SHL also has an interactive version. Check the instructions screen, and see [which version you have](versions.md).

## How long is the SHL Verify G+ test?

36 minutes allowed. SHL gives an average testing time of 32 to 36 minutes.

## How many questions are there?

Up to 30: 10 numerical, 10 deductive and 10 inductive, according to SHL's fact sheet. Because the test is adaptive, SHL describes 30 as the maximum.

## Is it adaptive?

Yes. SHL describes Verify G+ as using computer adaptive technology, so the questions depend on how you are doing. See [how scoring works](scoring.md#adaptive-scoring-not-a-raw-count).

## Can I use a calculator?

SHL's candidate support pages say you may use a calculator for numerical or calculation tests unless you are told otherwise. Another SHL support page says that if the test shows an on-screen calculator, that is the one to use. Pen and paper are fine. Follow the instructions in your invitation.

## Are you expected to finish every question?

The time is tight on purpose, and SHL's average testing time of 32 to 36 minutes shows most people use almost all of it. Aim to answer every question, keep to the pace in the [timing guide](timing.md), and do not let one item eat several minutes.

## Is there negative marking?

SHL does not publish a penalty for wrong answers on its Verify G+ pages. Answer every question you reach, but answer carefully rather than at random.

## What is the pass mark?

There is no published pass mark. Each employer decides what result it needs, and results are compared with a group of other test takers, so the same answers can mean different things against different groups.

## What is a good score?

A percentile tells you how you compare with the employer's chosen group. 50 means better than half. See [what counts as a good score](scoring.md#what-counts-as-a-good-score).

## Can I take it on my phone?

SHL describes Verify G+ as designed for unsupervised use but does not say on the fact sheet which devices it supports. SHL's interactive practice test is described as working on any device. Check your invitation, and use a computer with a reliable connection if you can.

## Can I retake the test?

That depends on the employer. SHL's candidate support describes "cooling periods" that recruiters can set to limit repeat attempts. If you need a retake because of a technical problem, contact the employer.

## How do I prepare for the SHL general ability test?

Learn a method for each of the three question types, practise each one, then do timed runs. The [study plans](study-plans.md) set this out day by day.

## Is there an official practice test?

Yes. SHL hosts free [practice tests and example questions](https://www.shl.com/shldirect/en/practice-tests/), including a general ability test and single-ability tests. SHL notes that the practice difficulty may not match the real test.

## Where can I practise for free?

SHL's own practice site, the items in this repo, and the [free SHL Verify G+ practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/shl-general-ability?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=shl-verify-g-plus), which mixes all three question types under a timer.

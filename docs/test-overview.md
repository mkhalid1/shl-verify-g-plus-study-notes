# The SHL Verify G+ test explained

SHL Verify G+ is SHL's general ability test. It measures general mental ability, often called "G", through three kinds of question: numerical, deductive and inductive reasoning. Employers use it to compare applicants' problem-solving and reasoning, at every level from school leavers to senior hires. This page covers what the test is, how it is built and what that means for how you prepare.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | SHL |
| Questions | Up to 30: 10 numerical, 10 deductive, 10 inductive |
| Time allowed | 36 minutes. SHL gives an average testing time of 32 to 36 minutes. |
| Format | Multiple choice, computer adaptive |
| Sittings | One |
| Where | Online, designed for unsupervised use |
| Job levels | SHL says the adaptive design makes it suitable for all job levels and roles |
| Pass mark | None published. Each employer decides what result it needs. |

Source: [SHL Verify G+ fact sheet](https://www.shl.com/assets/documents/rebranded-assets/product-factsheet-verify-g-plus.pdf), checked 24 November 2026.

## The three abilities

| Ability | What SHL says it measures | In practice | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Numerical | Solving problems with numerical data and interpreting charts, graphs and tables | Percentages, ratios, averages and comparisons from business data | [Numerical reasoning](sections/numerical-reasoning.md) |
| Deductive | Drawing logical conclusions, judging arguments, completing scenarios with incomplete information | "Which must be true", ordering puzzles, strengthen and weaken | [Deductive reasoning](sections/deductive-reasoning.md) |
| Inductive | Detecting patterns and inferring rules, using shapes and figures only | Find the rule in a series of figures and apply it | [Inductive reasoning](sections/inductive-reasoning.md) |

Your result comes back as an overall general ability score, and SHL's sample reports also show a separate score for each of the three abilities. See [how scoring works](scoring.md).

## How the test is built

Two design choices shape the whole experience.

**It is adaptive.** SHL describes Verify G+ as using "computer adaptive technology". The questions you get depend on how you are doing, so two candidates do not see the same test. SHL lists the benefits as a shorter test, the ability to run it unsupervised, and a more precise score. The fact sheet gives a "maximum number of questions" of 30.

**It is meant for unsupervised use.** You take it at home on your own device. The employer may check your result later by other means. SHL's sample report advises employers to confirm unsupervised results with other evidence, such as interviews, work simulations or qualifications. So an inflated score from outside help tends not to last.

What this means for you:

- You cannot learn the questions in advance. You can learn the question *types* and a method for each.
- Feeling that the questions are getting harder is normal in an adaptive test, and is not a sign you are doing badly. That is our reading of how adaptive tests work, not an SHL statement.
- Pace matters. 36 minutes for up to 30 questions is about 72 seconds each.

## Which version you have

SHL runs more than one general ability test, and invitations do not always use the exact product name. Verify G+ is the multiple-choice version described here. SHL also offers an interactive, mobile-friendly general ability practice test that uses drag and drop instead of multiple choice. The skills are the same, but the screens look different. See [which SHL general ability test you have](versions.md) for how to tell.

## The one habit that runs through the whole test

Work only from what is on the screen. Numerical answers come from the table, not from what seems realistic. Deductive answers must follow from the facts, however odd the facts are. Inductive answers follow the rule you can see in every frame, not a cleverer one you imagine. Most lost points in all three sections come from adding something that is not there.

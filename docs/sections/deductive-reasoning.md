# Deductive reasoning

**In one line:** you are given a few facts or rules and pick the conclusion that must follow, or judge which statement makes an argument stronger or weaker.

Deductive reasoning is one of the three abilities in Verify G+, with ten of the 30 questions. SHL's fact sheet describes it as drawing logical conclusions from information provided, identifying strengths and weaknesses of arguments, and completing scenarios using incomplete information.

## What it measures

Deductive questions test whether you can stay strictly inside the information you are given. Three skills come up again and again:

- **Must versus might.** A conclusion that *could* be true is not good enough. The right answer is the one that *has to* be true if the facts are true.
- **Ordering and scheduling.** Rules such as "the toner arrives the day after the paper" or "Safety must be first or last" that you combine to fix who goes where.
- **Weighing arguments.** Spotting which new fact would most damage, or most support, a claim. This is where workplace judgement shows.

None of it needs specialist knowledge. Everything you need is on the screen.

## How the questions look

| Type | What you get | What you choose |
|---|---|---|
| Must be true | Three to five short facts | The one option that follows for certain |
| Ordering or scheduling | People, days or slots plus a set of rules | The option that must be true once the rules are combined |
| Argument strength | A short argument or claim | The option that most weakens it, most strengthens it, or that it depends on |

There are five answer options. Often three are plainly wrong, and the real choice is between one statement that *must* be true and one that only *might* be.

## A method that works

1. **Read the question line first.** "Must be true", "most weakens" and "relies on" are different tasks.
2. **Turn each fact into a short note.** "All A are B" becomes A → B. "Not on Monday" becomes a cross in a grid.
3. **For ordering problems, draw the slots.** Write the days or positions in a row and fill in fixed facts first. Then try each remaining rule.
4. **Test each option against the notes, one at a time.** Ask: "Is there any way the facts could all be true and this option false?" If yes, it is not a must.
5. **For arguments, find the gap.** Name what the argument takes for granted. The best weakener attacks that gap. The best strengthener closes it.
6. **Do not bring in outside knowledge.** If the facts say red cars do not exist in a town, accept it, however odd it sounds.

## Common forms to know

| Facts | Valid conclusion | Tempting but invalid |
|---|---|---|
| All A are B. X is A. | X is B. | |
| All A are B. X is B. | Nothing about whether X is A. | X is A. |
| If P then Q. Not Q. | Not P. | |
| If P then Q. Q. | Nothing about P. | P. |
| No A are B. All C are B. | No C are A. | Some C are A. |
| Some A are B. | Some B are A. | All A are B. |

Learn the two invalid rows in particular. "All A are B, X is B, so X is A" is the single most common deduction error.

## Traps that cost points

- **Reversing a rule.** "Everyone who finished the course got a badge" does not mean everyone with a badge finished the course.
- **Picking what is likely instead of what is certain.** In a "must be true" question, probably true is wrong.
- **Stopping at the first arrangement.** In ordering problems, an option that is true in the first arrangement you find may be false in another valid one. Check the alternatives before you commit.
- **Confusing need with presence.** "A long meeting needs a manager's approval" says nothing about whether a manager attends.
- **Choosing a weakener that is merely negative.** The best weakener attacks the link between evidence and conclusion. A fact that is bad news but unrelated to the reasoning does not weaken it.
- **Reading extra words into the facts.** "Some" means at least one, possibly all. It does not mean "not all".

## Timing

With about a minute per question across the whole test, a short "must be true" item should take 40 to 60 seconds, which buys time for ordering problems that can take two minutes. As our guidance: if an ordering grid has not settled after two attempts, test each option directly against the rules instead of solving the whole grid. You only need to know which option has to be true.

## Practice items

These 12 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from Verify G+ or any SHL material. Each has five options and one best answer. Use paper for the ordering items.

### Set 1: Must be true

**Item 1.** All members of the audit team are qualified accountants. Some qualified accountants work part-time. Priya is on the audit team. Which of the following must be true?

A. Priya works part-time.
B. Priya is a qualified accountant.
C. Some members of the audit team work part-time.
D. Everyone who works part-time is an accountant.
E. Priya does not work part-time.

**Item 2.** If the shipment clears customs by Tuesday, it will be in the warehouse on Wednesday. The shipment was not in the warehouse on Wednesday. Which of the following must be true?

A. The shipment cleared customs on Wednesday.
B. The shipment did not clear customs by Tuesday.
C. The warehouse was closed on Wednesday.
D. The shipment has been lost.
E. The shipment will arrive on Thursday.

**Item 3.** Every employee who completed the safety course received a badge. Tom has a badge. What can be concluded about Tom?

A. Tom completed the safety course.
B. Tom did not complete the safety course.
C. Tom may or may not have completed the safety course.
D. Everyone with a badge completed the safety course.
E. Tom works in a safety role.

**Item 4.** No product launched this year was tested on the old platform. Every product in the beta programme was tested on the old platform. Which of the following must be true?

A. No product in the beta programme was launched this year.
B. Some products in the beta programme were launched this year.
C. All products launched this year were in the beta programme.
D. The old platform has been retired.
E. Every product not in the beta programme was launched this year.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 4</summary>

**Item 1: B.** Priya is on the audit team, and everyone on the audit team is a qualified accountant. *Why not A or C:* "some qualified accountants work part-time" does not say any of them are on the audit team. *Why not E:* she might work part-time, and nothing rules it out.

**Item 2: B.** This is "if P then Q, not Q, so not P". Clearing customs by Tuesday would have put the shipment in the warehouse on Wednesday. It was not there, so it did not clear by Tuesday. *Why not A, C, D or E:* each could be true, but none follows from the facts.

**Item 3: C.** The rule runs one way: completing the course leads to a badge. It does not say a badge can only come from the course. Tom could have earned his badge another way. *Why not A:* that reverses the rule, the most common trap in this section. *Why not D:* this is the reversed rule stated as a fact.

**Item 4: A.** Every beta product was tested on the old platform, and no product launched this year was. So no beta product can have launched this year. *Why not B:* it contradicts the facts. *Why not E:* products outside the beta could have launched in any year.

</details>

### Set 2: Ordering and scheduling

**Item 5.** Five deliveries arrive on five days, Monday to Friday, one each day: paper, toner, chairs, desks and screens. The toner arrives the day after the paper. The desks arrive on Friday. The chairs arrive before the paper. The screens do not arrive on Monday. Which of the following must be true?

A. The paper arrives on Tuesday.
B. The screens arrive on Thursday.
C. The chairs arrive on Monday.
D. The toner arrives on Wednesday.
E. The screens arrive before the paper.

**Item 6.** Ali, Bea, Cal and Dev sit at four desks in a row, numbered 1 to 4 from left to right. Bea sits at desk 2 or desk 3. Dev does not sit at either end. Cal sits immediately to the left of Bea. Which of the following must be true?

A. Dev sits at desk 2.
B. Ali sits next to Dev.
C. Bea sits at desk 3.
D. Cal sits next to Dev.
E. Ali sits at desk 1.

**Item 7.** In a sales contest, Uma sold more than Vic. Wes sold less than Vic but more than Xan. Yara sold more than Uma. Which of the following must be true?

A. Wes sold more than Uma.
B. Xan sold the least of the five.
C. Vic came second.
D. Yara sold twice as much as Xan.
E. Uma came first.

**Item 8.** A training day has four sessions in four consecutive slots, numbered 1 to 4: Excel, Writing, Negotiation and Safety. Safety must be in slot 1 or slot 4. Writing comes immediately after Excel. Negotiation is not in slot 2. Which of the following must be true?

A. Safety is in slot 4.
B. Excel is in slot 1 or slot 2.
C. Negotiation is in slot 1.
D. Writing is in slot 3.
E. Safety and Negotiation are in neighbouring slots.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 5 to 8</summary>

**Item 5: C.** Desks fill Friday. Monday cannot be the screens (a rule), the paper (the chairs come before it) or the toner (the paper comes before it). So Monday is the chairs. *Why not A, B, D or E:* the remaining days allow two orders, paper Tuesday, toner Wednesday, screens Thursday, or screens Tuesday, paper Wednesday, toner Thursday. Each of A, B, D and E is true in one order and false in the other.

**Item 6: B.** Bea is at desk 2 or 3, and Dev is not at an end, so Bea and Dev take desks 2 and 3 between them. Cal and Ali take desks 1 and 4. Cal must be immediately left of Bea, so Cal is at desk 1, Bea at 2, Dev at 3 and Ali at 4. Ali, at desk 4, sits next to Dev at desk 3. *Why not D:* Cal sits next to Bea, not Dev.

**Item 7: B.** Put the facts in order: Yara above Uma, Uma above Vic, Vic above Wes, Wes above Xan. So the full order is Yara, Uma, Vic, Wes, Xan. Xan sold least. *Why not C:* Vic is third. *Why not D:* the facts give an order, not amounts.

**Item 8: B.** Three orders fit the rules: Safety, Excel, Writing, Negotiation; or Negotiation, Excel, Writing, Safety; or Excel, Writing, Negotiation, Safety. Excel is in slot 2, 2 and 1, so always in slot 1 or 2. *Why not A:* the first order has Safety in slot 1. *Why not D:* the third order has Writing in slot 2. *Why not E:* in the first order Safety is in slot 1 and Negotiation in slot 4.

</details>

### Set 3: Arguments

**Item 9.** "Our new onboarding course works. Staff who took it last year received higher performance ratings than staff who did not." Which of the following, if true, most weakens this argument?

A. The course was optional, and most of the staff who chose it were already rated highly before they took it.
B. The new course costs less than the old one.
C. Performance ratings are given once a year.
D. Some staff took the course twice.
E. The course includes a module on company values.

**Item 10.** "The city should keep its central library open until 9pm, because 70% of library members surveyed said they would visit more often in the evening." Which of the following, if true, most strengthens this argument?

A. When a similar library in a nearby town stayed open until 9pm, evening visits rose sharply and stayed high for a year.
B. Most library members are students.
C. Only 50 members answered the survey.
D. Library staff prefer daytime shifts.
E. Lighting and heating cost more in the evening.

**Item 11.** "We should open a second café on the high street, because our first café has a queue out of the door every lunchtime." Which assumption does this argument depend on?

A. Many of the people queueing would use a second café nearby.
B. Lunch is the busiest time for every café.
C. The first café's kitchen is too small.
D. Rents on the high street are falling.
E. The first café sells hot food as well as drinks.

**Item 12.** "Sales of our walking boots rose 30% in the month after we redesigned the product page, so the redesign caused the rise." Which of the following, if true, most weakens this argument?

A. The redesign took three months to complete.
B. The same month, the largest rival brand stopped selling walking boots in the country.
C. The new product page uses larger photos.
D. Walking boots are the shop's best-selling product.
E. Some customers said they liked the old page better.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 9 to 12</summary>

**Item 9: A.** The argument assumes the course caused the higher ratings. If high performers chose the course, the ratings gap existed before the course, so the evidence no longer shows the course works. *Why not B or E:* true or not, they say nothing about whether the course improves performance.

**Item 10: A.** The argument rests on what members *say* they would do. Evidence that people actually came in the evening, at a similar library, closes the gap between stated intention and behaviour. *Why not C, D or E:* each one weakens the case or argues against it. *Why not B:* it describes members but does not support evening opening.

**Item 11: A.** The queue only supports a second café if the demand behind it would move there. If the queueing customers only want this café, for example because it is next to their office, a second site gains nothing. *Why not C:* a small kitchen might explain the queue, but the argument does not depend on it.

**Item 12: B.** If the main rival left the market that month, its customers needed new boots, which offers another explanation for the rise. That breaks the link between the redesign and the sales. *Why not E:* a few customers' opinions do not explain the sales figures.

</details>

- **11 to 12:** strong. Keep the "common forms" table fresh and focus on your weakest other section.

- **7 or fewer:** go back to the method. Write each fact as a note before looking at the options, and test every option with "could this be false?"

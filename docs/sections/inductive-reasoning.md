# Inductive reasoning

**In one line:** you look at a series or grid of shapes, work out the rule that changes them, and pick the figure that comes next or fills the gap.

Inductive reasoning is the third ability in Verify G+, with ten of the 30 questions. SHL's fact sheet calls these questions "completely non-verbal": they use only shapes and figures and measure the ability to detect patterns and infer the rules behind them.

## What it measures

Inductive questions ask you to go from examples to a rule. Nobody tells you the rule. You have to find it, check it against every frame, and apply it one step further. The skills are:

- **Isolating features.** A frame may have a position, a shape, a colour, a count and a rotation. Each can follow its own rule.
- **Spotting the kind of change.** Moving, rotating, growing, alternating, cycling, combining.
- **Holding two rules at once.** Harder items run two or three independent rules in the same frame.

SHL's report wording describes this as working with incomplete information and creating solutions to novel problems from first principles.

## How the questions look

Most items show a row of frames with the next one missing, or a grid with one cell empty, and five candidate figures. SHL's own example questions show "a logical sequence of five boxes" and ask which box completes it, chosen from options A to E.

The real test uses drawn figures. In these notes we write the figures as text symbols so that you can work through them anywhere. Read each frame left to right.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ● ○ | black dot, white dot |
| ■ □ | shaded cell, empty cell |
| ▲ | triangle |
| ↑ → ↓ ← ↗ ↘ ↙ ↖ | an arrow pointing that way |
| `●│` | a frame holding a dot and one bar |

## A method that works

1. **List the features.** Before guessing, write what can change: position, number, shape, shading, direction.
2. **Check one feature at a time across all frames.** Does the position move? By how much? Does the count go up?
3. **Name the rule in a few words.** "Moves one cell right and wraps." "Turns 90° clockwise." A rule you can say is a rule you can apply.
4. **Test the rule on every frame,** not just the first two. Plenty of patterns look like +1 for two frames and then turn out to be doubling.
5. **Predict the answer before looking at the options.** Then find the option that matches every feature.
6. **Eliminate by feature.** If the arrow must point down, cross out every option that does not. Then check the next feature on what is left.

## Traps that cost points

- **Fixing on one feature.** You get the position right and miss that the colour also alternates. Wrong options are built to catch exactly this.
- **Checking only two frames.** The rule has to hold for every step shown.
- **Ignoring wraparound.** Something moving right along a row often reappears on the left.
- **Mixing up clockwise and anticlockwise** for rotations. Say it out loud.
- **Steps that grow.** A hand that moves one place, then two, then three is common. If the gaps are not equal, look at the gaps themselves.
- **Overthinking.** If one simple rule explains every frame, it is the answer. Do not look for a second rule that nothing requires.

## Timing

Inductive items are usually quicker than numerical ones once the rule clicks, which makes them the place to bank time. As our guidance: aim for 45 to 60 seconds each. If no rule has appeared after a minute, eliminate the options that break any feature you are sure of, pick from what remains, and move on.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from Verify G+ or any SHL material. Each shows frames 1 to 5 and asks for frame 6 unless the item says otherwise.

### Set 1: One rule

**Item 1.** Each frame holds a number of triangles.

| Frame 1 | Frame 2 | Frame 3 | Frame 4 | Frame 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ▲ | ▲▲▲ | ▲▲▲▲▲ | ▲▲▲▲▲▲▲ | ? |

Which is frame 5?

A. 8 triangles &nbsp; B. 9 triangles &nbsp; C. 10 triangles &nbsp; D. 11 triangles &nbsp; E. 14 triangles

**Item 2.** A clock face with one hand. The table shows the hour the hand points to.

| Frame 1 | Frame 2 | Frame 3 | Frame 4 | Frame 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 1 | 3 | 6 | ? |

Which is frame 5?

A. 7 &nbsp; B. 8 &nbsp; C. 9 &nbsp; D. 10 &nbsp; E. 12

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 and 2</summary>

**Item 1: B, 9 triangles.** The counts go 1, 3, 5, 7: two more each frame. Next is 9. *Why not E:* 14 would be doubling 7, but the earlier frames do not double.

**Item 2: D, 10.** The hand moves 1 hour, then 2, then 3. Next it moves 4, from 6 to 10. *Why not C:* 9 assumes the hand keeps moving 3 hours, but the gaps grow by one each time.

</details>

### Set 2: Two rules at once

**Item 3.** A 2 × 2 grid with one dot. The table gives the corner and the dot's colour.

| Frame 1 | Frame 2 | Frame 3 | Frame 4 | Frame 5 | Frame 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| top left, black | top right, white | bottom right, black | bottom left, white | top left, black | ? |

A. top right, black &nbsp; B. top right, white &nbsp; C. bottom left, white &nbsp; D. top left, white &nbsp; E. bottom right, white

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, item 3</summary>

**Item 3: B, top right, white.** Two rules. Position: the dot moves one corner clockwise each frame (top left, top right, bottom right, bottom left, then round again). Colour: it alternates black, white, black, white. Frame 6 is one corner on from top left, which is top right, and follows a black frame, so it is white. *Why not A:* right corner, wrong colour.

</details>

### Set 3: Harder patterns

**Item 4.** Each frame shows an arrow and some dots. Read the changes between frames carefully.

| Frame 1 | Frame 2 | Frame 3 | Frame 4 | Frame 5 | Frame 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ↑ ● | → ● | → ●● | ↓ ●● | ↓ ●●● | ? |

A. ↓ ●●●● &nbsp; B. ← ●●● &nbsp; C. ← ●●●● &nbsp; D. ↑ ●●● &nbsp; E. ↓ ●●●

**Item 5.** A 3 × 3 grid of dot counts with the last cell missing. The same rule applies across every row.

| | Column 1 | Column 2 | Column 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Row 1 | ● | ●● | ●● |
| Row 2 | ●● | ●●● | ●●●●●● |
| Row 3 | ●●● | ●● | ? |

How many dots belong in the missing cell?

A. 1 &nbsp; B. 4 &nbsp; C. 5 &nbsp; D. 6 &nbsp; E. 9

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 4 and 5</summary>

**Item 4: B, ← ●●●.** The frames take turns between two changes. From frame 1 to 2 the arrow turns 90° clockwise. From 2 to 3 a dot is added. From 3 to 4 the arrow turns. From 4 to 5 a dot is added. So from 5 to 6 the arrow turns again, from down to left, and the dots stay at three. *Why not C:* that turns the arrow *and* adds a dot in one step.

**Item 5: D, 6.** In each row, column 3 is column 1 multiplied by column 2: 1 × 2 = 2, 2 × 3 = 6, so 3 × 2 = 6. *Why not C:* adding the first two columns gives 5 for row 3, but adding does not work for rows 1 and 2 (1 + 2 = 3, not 2).

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | B | D | B | B | D |

- **5:** good pattern sense. In the real test, use the time you save here on numerical items.
- **4:** most misses in set 2 and 3 come from finding one rule and stopping. Train the habit of listing every feature first.
- **3 or fewer:** do the items again slowly, writing each feature as its own row and filling in how it changes. Speed comes after the habit.

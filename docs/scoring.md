# How SHL Verify G+ scoring works

Short version: your answers are turned into an ability estimate, that estimate is compared with a group of other people who took the test, and the result is reported as a percentile. There is no universal pass mark. Each employer decides what result it wants and which comparison group to use.

One note on sources before we start. SHL publishes a fact sheet for Verify G+ but not a sample score report for it. The sample report it does publish is for **Verify Interactive G+**, which measures the same three abilities in an interactive format. We use that report below to show how SHL presents G+ results, and say so each time.

## Adaptive scoring, not a raw count

Verify G+ is adaptive. SHL says the computer adaptive design gives "a more precise test score" in a shorter test. In an adaptive test the questions you get depend on your earlier answers, so two people can answer the same number correctly and still get different results, because one of them answered harder questions. That is why there is no simple "you need 20 out of 30".

SHL does not publish the scoring model for Verify G+ on its public pages, so we do not describe it in more detail.

## Percentiles and comparison groups

SHL reports your result against a **comparison group**: a large group of people who took the test before. The sample Interactive G+ report gives each score as a percentile, for example "better than 68% of the people in this group".

| Report element | What it shows (sample Interactive G+ report) |
|---|---|
| General ability | One overall G+ percentile, based on all questions |
| Inductive, numerical, deductive | A percentile for each ability |
| Comparison group | The group you are compared with, named on the report, for example a general population or a composite group |
| Band wording | A plain-language label such as "average when compared to the comparison group" |

Two things follow:

1. **The same performance can give different percentiles** depending on the comparison group the employer chooses. A graduate group sets a higher bar than a general population group.
2. **Employers set their own cut-off.** SHL does not publish one, and any figure you see online is a guess.

## Other scores on the report

The sample report includes a technical section for trained users:

| Score type | What it is |
|---|---|
| Percentile | The share of the comparison group you scored better than |
| T-score | A standard score with a mean of 50 and a standard deviation of 10 |
| Sten | A ten-point standard score with a mean of 5.5 and a standard deviation of 2 |

You may never see these. Whether you get feedback at all depends on the employer.

## Unsupervised results and checking them

The sample report says plainly that if the tests were unsupervised, "there is a small possibility that these scores do not represent his actual level of ability". It then lists ways for the employer to confirm a result: other assessments such as work simulations, exam results and qualifications, and structured interview questions about related skills.

In plain terms: the score is meant to be your own work, and employers are advised to look for evidence that backs it up.

## How long a result lasts

SHL's older sample Verify candidate report says results "remain valid for about 12 to 18 months". Some employers will reuse a recent result, others will ask you to sit the test again. Ask if it matters to you.

## What counts as a good score

There is no published pass mark. As a rough guide to reading a percentile: 50 means you did better than half the comparison group, and 70 means better than seven in ten. Where the employer draws its line, and how much weight it gives the test next to interviews and other stages, is its own decision. The useful aim is simple: answer as many questions correctly as you can in the time, without guessing wildly early on.

## Should you guess?

SHL does not publish a penalty for wrong answers on its Verify G+ pages, and we have not found one in SHL's own material. Our advice: never leave an item unanswered if the test lets you move on, but do not rush into blind guesses on early questions. In an adaptive test, careful answers at the start help you reach the questions that show your real level.

## Practise and track your score

Track your scores by ability, not only overall. The [free SHL Verify G+ practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/shl-general-ability?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=shl-verify-g-plus) breaks results down so you can see which of the three abilities to work on next.

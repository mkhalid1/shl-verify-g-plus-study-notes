# Glossary

Terms you will meet in SHL's material, your invitation and these notes.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Adaptive test** | A test that picks each question based on how you answered earlier ones, so different people see different questions. Verify G+ is computer adaptive. |
| **Base value** | The starting figure in a percentage change. The change is divided by this number. |
| **Comparison group** | The group of earlier test takers your result is compared with. Also called a norm group. The employer chooses it. |
| **Conversion rate** | In numerical questions, the share of visitors or leads who go on to buy. Orders = visitors × conversion rate. |
| **Deductive reasoning** | Drawing conclusions that must follow from the facts given. One of the three Verify G+ abilities. |
| **G, general mental ability** | A broad measure of reasoning and problem-solving ability. SHL says Verify G+ measures it. |
| **Inductive reasoning** | Finding the rule behind a pattern and applying it. In Verify G+ these questions use only shapes and figures. |
| **Interactive G+** | SHL's interactive general ability format, which uses drag and drop and similar actions rather than multiple choice. |
| **Modus tollens** | The valid form "if P then Q, not Q, so not P". |
| **Numerical reasoning** | Making decisions and inferences from numbers in tables and charts. One of the three Verify G+ abilities. |
| **Percentile** | The share of the comparison group you scored better than. A percentile of 70 means better than 70% of the group. |
| **Sten** | A standard score on a 1 to 10 scale, with a mean of 5.5 and a standard deviation of 2. |
| **Strengthen, weaken** | Argument questions that ask which new fact most supports or most damages a claim. |
| **T-score** | A standard score with a mean of 50 and a standard deviation of 10. |
| **Unsupervised test** | A test you take on your own, without an invigilator. Verify G+ is designed for this. |
| **Weighted average** | An average where each group counts in proportion to its size. Needed when groups differ in size. |
| **Wraparound** | In inductive patterns, an element that leaves one edge and reappears at the opposite edge. |
